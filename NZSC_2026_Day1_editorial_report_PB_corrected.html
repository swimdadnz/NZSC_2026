2026 New Zealand Short Course Swimming Championships · Day 1

The kids rewrote the age-group book — and the senior fields hit Beijing pace.

Seven able-bodied New Zealand age-group records fell across the first day at Parakiore, led by a spectacular double from 13-year-old April Lin. Meanwhile 14 individual swims dipped under the World Aquatics “B” standards used by Swimming NZ for World Cup selection — with two of the narrowest calls coming in the 100 IM.

What Day 1 says

The headline is not an open-record assault. It is depth in the next generation. Four of the seven distinct national age records came from 13–14-year-olds, and April Lin's 200 breast moved a long-standing age mark by more than 2.6% in a single day. At the senior end, the fields were strong enough to generate 14 B-standard performances, but several of the most interesting swims were about margins rather than medals: Blake Elliott made the men's 100 IM cut by two hundredths, Zyleika Pratt-Smith missed the women's cut by six hundredths, and the men's IM podium was separated by one-third of a second.

That combination — age-group records falling hard while senior qualification battles are decided in hundredths — is a useful tone-setter for the rest of the meet.

Stories behind the numbers

The performances and patterns that best capture Day 1.

April Lin's two-act 200 breast

The most emphatic age-group performance was not merely a record — it was a same-day record progression. Lin went 2:31.69 in the morning, 1.24s inside the old 13-year mark, then dropped another 2.79s in the open final to 2:28.90.

Three men, 0.33 seconds

The men's 100 IM open podium compressed into one-third of a second. Fraser Walker won in 54.58, Blake Elliott was 0.23s back, and Roman Anderson was another tenth behind.

A surname that kept appearing

Zhang was a recurring name through the programme: Kevin Zhang won the men's 200 breast in 2:07.49, Yeenok Zhang and Yi Fan Zhang appeared in the men's 100 back, and Nicole Zhang and Aaron Zhang appeared in the women's/men's 100 IM.

At least five distinct able-bodied Zhang swimmers appear in the Day 1 result set; this is descriptive only and makes no assumption about family relationships.

Coast owned both 4×100 free relays

Coast won the women's relay in 3:42.51 and the men's in 3:13.54. The men's team finished 3.03s clear of United, while Jasper Cornish produced the fastest men's flying split at 47.52.

William Tye turned the 1500 into a record swim

Tye won the men's 1500 in 15:26.42, taking 8.14s off the pre-meet 15-year NZ record. He was still only 6.00s outside the 15:20.42 international B benchmark.

The women's 200 breast sharpened at night

8 of the top 10 NZ-eligible finishers improved on their morning swim. Zyleika Pratt-Smith won in 2:24.78, just 0.52s ahead of Brearna Crawford.

Methodology and data notes

The report is generated from the retained row-level database. Total summaries are used as canonical swim rows to avoid duplicating athletes across Swimify's age-band summaries. Age-band summaries are retained for age-group medal allocation. Visitor status is inferred from Swimify's (V) marker and club country; multi-class rows are retained in the workbook but excluded from this report. The 800m women and 1500m men use a single Swimify round summary across morning/evening programme entries, so Day 1 assignment is secure although exact session attribution for each distance swimmer is not available from the current query. Record-setting swim counts update the applicable mark chronologically, so a later swim that is still under the pre-meet record but slower than the new same-day record is not counted again. PB analysis uses the original first-round Entry as the pre-meet PB and the fastest valid individual swim across all rounds on the day; the club comparison is displayed only for medal-winning clubs with at least five distinct Day 1 swimmers.
