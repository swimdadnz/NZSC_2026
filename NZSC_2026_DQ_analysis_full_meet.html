Where did the DQs happen?

A full-meet look at disqualification incidence by reconstructed lane and event across all five days of the 2026 New Zealand Short Course Championships. The pattern is concentrated: lane 1 stands out, technical events carry most of the DQs, and the overall rate fell markedly in the final two days.

Key takeaways

1
Lane 1 has the highest rate

9 DQs from 353 starts (2.55%), 2.55× the meet average.

2
400 IM is the clearest high-volume hotspot

Seven DQs from 155 starts (4.52%). The mixed 4×100 medley relay was higher at 12.50%, but that is only 2 DQs from 16 starts.

3
Technical events dominate the DQ count

400 IM, 200 back, 200 breast and 100 IM account for 23 of 36 DQs (63.9%) from 796 starts (22.1% of starts).

4
The rate eased late in the meet

Days 1–3: 1.33%. Days 4–5: 0.49% — about 63% lower.

5
Reasons cannot be inferred from the data

The DQ-code field is blank for all 36 DQs, so these patterns do not establish a technical or officiating cause.

200 back spread across multiple lanes

Its six DQs were recorded in lanes 0, 1, 1, 2, 3, 8, so the event signal was not confined to one lane.

Nine combined event groups had no DQs

9 of 24 event groups finished the meet without a single DQ.

Why might some events show higher DQ rates?

The data are consistent with greater rule complexity mattering, but they do not prove it. Events such as the 400 IM involve more turns, stroke transitions and stroke-specific requirements. Backstroke and breaststroke also have distinctive start, turn, body-position, pullout and touch rules. Because Swimify supplies no DQ reason codes here, this should be read as a plausible interpretation of the pattern rather than a causal finding.

Methodology and lane reconstruction
  • Source: final Swimify extraction after Day 5, using canonical Total summaries.
  • Scope: scheduled events 1–46. Multi-Class events and visitors are excluded. Para-classified athletes are retained if they raced in a standard programme event, because this analysis describes the lane/event environment of the main programme.
  • Starts exclude DNS; DQs remain in the denominator as starts. There were no DNFs in scope.
  • Swimify does not expose a literal lane-number field in this extraction. Lane numbers are reconstructed within each heat from lane-object ordering using the standard 10-lane seeding sequence 4, 5, 3, 6, 2, 7, 1, 8, 0, 9.
  • Event analysis combines corresponding men's and women's events. Mixed relays remain standalone event groups.
  • All 36 DQ-code fields are blank, so the analysis is descriptive and should not be used to infer officiating inconsistency or a specific infringement type.

* Reconstructed lane assignment. Full-meet DQ analysis prepared from the final 2026 NZ Short Course Championships Swimify extraction.
