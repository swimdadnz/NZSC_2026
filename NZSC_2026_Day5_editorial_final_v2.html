What Day 5 says

The last day was less about a single avalanche of records than about completing some of the meet's strongest individual narratives. Grayson Coulter's 23.11 in the 50 free gave him a sixth distinct 14-year NZ record event, before a 50.06 lead-off in North Shore's mixed 4x100 free relay became his 10th record-breaking swim of the championships. The relay split lowered his own 100 free mark from 50.10. William Tye's 8:07.90 in the 800 free added a second 15-year distance mark to the 1500 record he set on Day 1.

Alyssa Tapper supplied the biggest winning margin of the day, taking the women's 1500 in 16:07.91 - 32.28 seconds clear of second place and comfortably inside the Beijing B standard. Across the individual programme, Day 5 produced 233 PBs from 454 swimmer-event opportunities (51.3%), but the split was again pronounced: men were at 62.3% versus 40.3% for women. The meet closes with Coast on 24 Open golds / 43 Open medals and Phoenix on 30 age-group golds / 54 age-group medals.

The four editorial angles selected for publication.
Story 1 - Grayson Coulter

Ten record-breaking swims across six events

Coulter's 23.11 in the 14-year men's 50 free cut 0.34s from the pre-meet record and completed a remarkable spread of record events from 50 to 200 metres. Then, in the final session, he led off North Shore's mixed 4x100 free relay in 50.06, lowering his own 100 free record from 50.10. That was his 10th record-breaking swim across six distinct events over the five days.

Methodology and data notes

Day 5 is Sessions 9-10 and able-bodied events 39-46. Multi-class events and visitors are excluded from NZ records, Beijing counts, DQ/DNS audit and medal tallies. PB analysis includes able-bodied visitors for consistency with prior daily reports. Entry from the first round is treated as the swimmer's pre-meet PB, and the fastest valid Day 5 swim across heat, swim-off and final is compared with that original benchmark; updated later-round Entry values are not used as a new baseline. The pre-meet record benchmark is Swimming New Zealand's short-course record sheet as at 23 September 2026, with record progression applied chronologically through the meet. For Coulter's Day 5 mixed-relay lead-off, the applicable previous mark was his 50.10 from Day 2; his 50.06 lowered it by 0.04s. Swimming New Zealand's current records policy allows a first relay swimmer to establish an individual record, and its official championship wrap explicitly recognises this 50.06 lead-off as a 14-year NZ record. World Aquatics A/B standards are the official Beijing 2026 25m standards. For cumulative medal tables, visitor results are removed before allocating NZ medal positions.
